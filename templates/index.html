Your CAN buses. Anywhere.

can-hub puts the CAN interfaces of vehicles and machines on the network — through NAT, encrypted, authenticated — so the people and tools that need them can reach them from anywhere.

Get started GitHub

How it works

1

The device dials out

An agent next to the bus opens one outbound, encrypted connection to your hub. No public IP, no port forwarding, no VPN appliance.

2

The hub keeps the catalogue

Every interface gets a stable name — truck42/can0. Devices are identified by their TLS fingerprint, pinned on first contact. Impostors are rejected, not just logged.

3

Open it from anywhere

Stream a bus live, inject frames if you are allowed to, or mirror it into your own machine. Per-interface read/write permissions, enforced at the hub.

What people do with it

Fleet diagnostics

A truck on cellular, an engineer at the bench. Watch any bus in the fleet live and answer “what is that ECU doing?” without rolling a van.

Remote operations & upgrades in development

Request/response sequences run next to the bus, not across the WAN: the agent executes buffered steps at bus speed while your link only refills the buffer. Hours of round trips become minutes.

Your tools, unmodified

Mirror a remote bus into a local virtual interface and keep using candump, SavvyCAN, Wireshark or python-can as if the bus were plugged into your machine.

Drop-in for legacy setups

Existing socketcand clients connect to a local bridge and reach hub interfaces — the same protocol they already speak, now with authentication and permissions in front.

Secure by default, not by configuration

Zero-config identity

Keys generated on first start. The TLS fingerprint is the device identity.

Pinned on first use

Hub and devices pin each other. A changed fingerprint is a rejected connection.

Mutual TLS

Client certificates required on every encrypted transport.

Per-interface ACLs

Read and write granted per client and per bus, enforced at the hub.

Open source, with a commercial path
